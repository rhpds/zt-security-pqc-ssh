= Conclusion and Next Steps

Congratulations — you have configured an SSH server on Red Hat Enterprise Linux 10 to require hybrid post-quantum key exchange, using only the standard command-line tools you already work with every day.

You started with the threat that makes this work urgent: *Harvest Now, Decrypt Later* (HNDL). An attacker does not need to break your encryption today. They can capture your traffic now, store it, and decrypt it later once a capable quantum computer exists. You verified that RHEL 10 already prefers hybrid ML-KEM for key exchange, then made that preference mandatory on `node1`.

== What You've Learned

* You inspected the `DEFAULT` crypto policy and confirmed that OpenSSH offers `mlkem768x25519-sha256`.
* You used verbose SSH output to compare the default hybrid handshake with a forced classical-only handshake.
* You configured an `sshd` drop-in on `node1`, validated it, and confirmed hybrid clients still connect while classical-only clients fail to negotiate.

The lab changes key exchange only, not SSH host keys or user authentication.

== Where to Go From Here

This lab is part 1 of a 3-part Red Hat Summit session on post-quantum security. You have the SSH building blocks; here is how to take them further.

* *Apply SSH policy at scale.* Before rolling out an OpenSSH policy with a RHEL System Role or Ansible, verify that your clients support the required key exchange.
* *Look beyond SSH.* System-wide crypto policies affect many applications, not just OpenSSH. Review how the same policy influences TLS, your web servers, and other services, and plan which workloads need post-quantum protection first based on the confidentiality lifetime of their data.
* *Build a migration plan.* Identify the long-lived, high-value data in your environment — the records most exposed to HNDL — and prioritize moving those connections to post-quantum algorithms.
* *Explore RHEL 10 security hardening.* Continue with the broader RHEL 10 security hardening documentation to combine post-quantum cryptography with the rest of your defense-in-depth strategy.
